# Dynamic Models

## Where we are

Parts 1 and 2 held every body still. Part 1 showed that round a single body, space flowing in at the escape speed is
general relativity exactly. Part 2 added more bodies: a flow with no swirl, with space stretched across it, is general
relativity for any number of bodies at rest, to the first order beyond Newton.

But nothing in the universe is held still. The Moon goes round the Earth, the Earth round the Sun, and stars round each
other. Parts 3 and 4 let the bodies move.

## What changes when bodies move

![Two panels. Left, the Sun and the Earth held still: space flows in towards each, a dashed seam surrounds the Earth's region of the flow, and nothing changes with time. Right, the Earth moving round the Sun: an arrow, v, runs along its orbit from a faint copy showing where the Earth and its region were a little earlier to where they are now; the Earth carries its region of flow and its seam with it](images/movingbodies.svg)

With the bodies held still, the flow never changes: at each place, space always flows the same way at the same speed.
Once they move, three things change.

- **Each body carries its flow.** The space falling into the Earth falls into the Earth where it is now, not where it
  was. So the flow at any one place changes with time, as the Earth goes by.
- **The seams move.** The seam round the Earth's region of the flow goes round the Sun with the Earth, and a point on
  the Earth's orbit is crossed by it twice a year, as the Earth comes and goes.
- **There is no frame where everything is still.** Part 1 measured everything in the body's own frame, and there was
  nothing to choose: with one body there is no preferred frame. With bodies moving, no single frame has them all at
  rest. Two bodies going round each other in a circle are still in a frame that turns with them, but that is a
  special case.

General relativity has effects that come only from motion, and the model will have to find them. A gyroscope carried
round the Sun turns slowly, by an amount that depends on its motion through the Sun's field: de Sitter's precession,
19.2 milliarcseconds a year for the Earth and Moon, which lunar laser ranging measures to better than 1%. Light passing
a moving body is bent by an amount that depends on the body's velocity, which radio telescopes measured as Jupiter
passed close to a distant quasar in 2002.

## Two steps

The change is taken in two steps.

- **Part 3: bodies on fixed paths.** The bodies are made to move on paths that are given: the Earth on a circle round
  the Sun, the Moon on its orbit. Their flows move with them, and the tests follow light, clocks and test particles
  through that moving flow. The question is what flow a moving body carries.
- **[Part 4](../part4/bodies-moved-by-the-flow.md): bodies moved by the flow.** The bodies are no longer given their
  paths. Each moves by the flow, its own included, and the question becomes whether the bodies' own motion comes out
  right: the orbit of a binary star, and whether the pair as a whole stays put.

## The tests ahead

Each page from here follows the same pattern as Part 2: a test the last model fails, a new model, and the tests it
passes. The tests, roughly in the order they will come:

| Test | What is measured | How well |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Newton's gravity round a moving body | a ball let go near the moving Earth falls straight down | everyday mechanics |
| Lunar laser ranging, with the Moon's own orbit | the Earth–Moon distance as the Moon goes round | to millimetres |
| Light past the Sun, with the Earth moving | the bending of radio signals grazing the Sun | Cassini, to 0.01 milliarcseconds |
| Light past a moving Jupiter | the bending's dependence on Jupiter's velocity | radio telescopes, 2002 |
| De Sitter precession | the turning of the Moon's orbit as the pair goes round the Sun | 19.2 mas a year, to under 1% |
| A binary's orbit, its bodies moving by the flow (Part 4) | how fast the orbit turns, for comparable masses | binary pulsars |
| The centre of a binary (Part 4) | stays put: the pair doesn't push itself along | momentum conservation |
| The Nordtvedt effect (Part 4) | a bound pair falls like a single body | lunar laser ranging |

The next page starts with the first of them.
