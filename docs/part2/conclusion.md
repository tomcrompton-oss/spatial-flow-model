# Conclusion

## Where we started

Part 1 ended with a single body, where flowing space is general relativity exactly. A second body raised a problem
that one body never did: the bodies' potentials say how fast space must flow, but nothing says which way. At the point
between two equal bodies, where their pulls cancel, space must still move fast, and in no particular direction.

## What we built

The [hollow sphere](hollow-sphere.md), with no pull inside but a deep potential, showed the problem at its simplest.
It also gave a rule for places where space flows two ways at once: something crossing one changes reference frame,
keeping its velocity relative to it, so that its energy is the same on both sides.

The [potential flow](potential-flow.md) chose the direction the way one body already does: space flows with no
swirl, down the paths of things falling from rest far away. That gives each body a region of its own, and seams where
the regions meet, crossed by the hollow sphere's rule. It gives Newton's gravity and general relativity's clocks
exactly. It failed the first test beyond them, lunar laser ranging.

[Curved space](curved-space.md) fixed that without changing anything else: the same flow, the same clocks, and space
stretched across the flow by $u^2/c^2$. That is not a guess. [Deriving Curved Space](deriving-curved-space.md) shows
it is what general relativity gives for bodies at rest, with time kept by clocks falling from far away. For one body
it is flat space in other coordinates, so Part 1 stands; with more, it carries the bodies' effects on each other.
[Multiple Bodies](multiple-bodies.md) takes it to three.

## The scorecard

Each model on each test. The first two are the obvious ways of combining two flows, from

*(Lunar ranging: the term that follows the Moon's angle from the Sun, less general relativity's. Light past Jupiter:
the bending with the Sun and Jupiter together less each alone, for light passing 1 Jupiter radius out, which general
relativity makes 0.25 µas. Blank: not run. The along-g flow keeps clocks held still right, since its speed is the potential's;
for a moving clock the flow's direction matters too, and that wasn't run.)*

Only the last model passes everything, and it passes as general relativity does: for bodies at rest, to the first
order beyond Newton, it is general relativity, written as flowing space.

## What is left

Two things.

The first is an open question about the flow itself. Where space meets itself, at the hollow sphere's walls or at the
seams between bodies, the rule says how things cross, but not what the space does: it flows into a seam, or out of
one, from both sides at once. The flow describes how space moves, not where it comes from or goes.

The second is the bodies. Every test in Part 2 held them still. Real bodies move: the Moon round the Earth, the Earth
round the Sun, two stars round each other. Moving bodies carry their flows with them, and that is where the next tests
are.
