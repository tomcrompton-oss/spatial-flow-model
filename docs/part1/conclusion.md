# Conclusion

## Where we started

Part 1 began with a simple observation. A clock held still at some point in a gravitational field runs slow, by
general relativity's time dilation. A clock moving at the escape speed for that point runs slow by special relativity's
time dilation, and by exactly the same amount. That could be a coincidence. The [Hypothesis](hypothesis.md) took it
literally: space itself flows inward at the escape speed, so a clock holding still is really moving through its space
at that speed, and its clock runs slow for the ordinary special relativistic reason.

## What we built

From that one idea, and the rules of [Newton and special relativity](mechanics/index.md) applied to things moving
through flowing space, we built a model, and a simulation of particles and light moving step by step through it. We
then put it through [the four classic tests](tests/index.md) of general relativity:

- [the gravitational redshift](tests/redshift.md), which it gives exactly;
- [the bending of light](tests/bending.md), the full deflection, not the half that Newton's gravity gives;
- [the precession of Mercury](tests/precession.md), its 43″ a century;
- [the Shapiro time delay](tests/shapiro.md).

The model would have explained every one of them, and in each case it agrees with exact general relativity, not
just with the approximations used at the time, to the accuracy of the simulation.

It goes further. Close to a [black hole](black-holes.md), where general relativity is hardest to picture, the model
gives the same answers: the falling observer's clock, the distant observer's view of the fall, the probe's clock on
each leg. With a twist added to the flow, it matches a [rotating black hole](rotating-black-holes.md) too, in the tests we ran, and run
backwards it gives [white holes](white-holes.md). For a single, non-rotating body, the model doesn't approximate general relativity.
It *is* general relativity.

## What we found

The reason is that we have rediscovered Gullstrand–Painlevé coordinates. In the early 1920s Allvar Gullstrand and Paul
Painlevé each noticed that Schwarzschild's solution of Einstein's equations can be written as flat space with space flowing
inward at the escape speed. Hamilton and Lisle's river model, on the [rotating black holes](rotating-black-holes.md)
page, is the same picture.

But we have come at it from the other side. Gullstrand–Painlevé coordinates start from general relativity, with its
curved spacetime and field equations, and pick out one special way of writing its solution for a single body. We
started from Newton and special relativity, with one physical idea added, and arrived at the same place. The flowing
space that general relativity allows as a choice of coordinates is, here, the starting point.

## The elephant in the room

So do we have a complete alternative view of general relativity, one that needs nothing more than Newton, special
relativity and flowing space?

Not yet. There is an elephant in the room that we have ignored so far. Everything in Part 1 has been about a single
body: one sun, one black hole, with space flowing straight into it. What happens when we introduce a second massive
body? Each body's flow on its own is simple. How the two combine is not, and that is where
[Part 2](../part2/two-body-problem.md) begins.
