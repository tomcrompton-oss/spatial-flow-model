# The Four Clocks

Time dilation in flowing space comes down to one rule: a clock runs slow by special relativity's factor for its speed
*through the space it is in*,

$$
\frac{d\tau}{dt} = \sqrt{1 - \frac{w^2}{c^2}}
$$

not for its speed as anyone else sees it. Four clocks round a single body, with space falling into it, show what
that means:

![Four clocks round a body with space falling into it. Clock 1 is far away, at rest in still space. Clock 2 falls in from far away with the space, so it is at rest in its space (w = 0). Clock 3 is held still on the surface, so space falls past it and it moves up through its space at w = u. Clock 4 has been thrown up from the surface at the escape speed, and moves up through its space at about 2u](images/fourclocks.svg)

## Clock 1: far away, at rest

Far from the body, space is at rest, and so is clock 1. It moves through no space, and its time is the base time the
others are compared with: the time $t$ in the rule above, which these pages call the flow's time.

Of course, we could have chosen an observer moving at any speed. There is no preferred frame of reference, and
special relativity works the same for all of them. We choose one at rest relative to the body because it makes the
flow, and everything else, simplest to describe.

## Clock 2: falling in from far away

Clock 2 was let go from rest far away, and has been falling ever since. But space is falling too, at exactly the
speed a clock falling from rest far away has: the escape speed, $u = \sqrt{2GM/r}$. So clock 2 moves *with* its space.
Relative to the space around it, it isn't moving at all, $w = 0$, and it has no time dilation. However fast it falls,
and however deep it gets, it keeps the same time as clock 1.

## Clock 3: held still on the surface

Clock 3 sits on the body's surface, at rest as far as anyone on the surface can tell. But space is falling past it
at $u$, so relative to its space it is moving *upward* at $u$, and by the rule it runs slow:

$$
\sqrt{1 - \frac{u^2}{c^2}} = \sqrt{1 - \frac{2GM}{rc^2}}
$$

which is exactly general relativity's time dilation for a clock held still at $r$ (see the
[Hypothesis](../hypothesis.md)). On the Earth's surface, $u$ is 11.2 km/s, and clock 3 loses 60 microseconds a day
against clock 1.

## Clock 4: escaping

Clock 4 has been thrown straight up from the surface at the escape speed. Relative to the ground it moves up at $u$,
but the ground is itself moving up through space at $u$. The two speeds add relativistically, so clock 4 moves up
through its space at

$$
w = \frac{u + u}{1 + u^2/c^2} \approx 2u
$$

and runs slower still. On the Earth it starts out losing 240 microseconds a day, four times as much as clock 3, since
the dilation goes as the speed squared. As it climbs it slows down, the space around it falls more slowly, and its
speed through space falls away. Far out it comes to rest, in space that is at rest, and runs at clock 1's rate again.

## Falling isn't enough

Clock 2 has no time dilation, but that doesn't mean every falling clock is free of it. Drop a clock from the top of a
tower: until the moment it is let go it is clock 3, held still and moving up through its space at the speed of the
space falling past it. Letting go doesn't change that. It starts its fall still moving up through its space, and
although it then speeds up, it never quite catches the space falling around it. A falling clock's time dilation
depends on where it was let go: the higher it started, the less it has. Only a clock that started from rest far away, where
space is at rest too, falls with no dilation at all.

## Clocks far apart

Comparing clocks that are far apart depends on how the distant ones are set, which is a choice: these pages use the
flow's time, the time clocks 1 and 2 keep. When two clocks meet, their readings can be compared directly, and those
comparisons are the same in flowing space as in general relativity: a clock carried up a tower and back down again
comes back with the same reading in both. The [Black Holes](../black-holes.md) page uses
signals sent between distant clocks to make the same point close to a black hole.
